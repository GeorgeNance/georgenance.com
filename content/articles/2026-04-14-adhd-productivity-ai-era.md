Six years ago, I wrote about how to be productive as an ADHD programmer. A lot of things have changed since then. I got married, I changed jobs, and this little thing called AI kinda took over the world.

AI tools have been both the best and worst thing to happen to ADHD programmers. They are fantastic for doing more with less effort. I can accomplish tasks that used to take hours in minutes now. But they also make it harder to step away from the screen, which is often what ADHD brains need to reset.

I know AI is a hot topic right now and there are arguments about whether it's a net positive or negative for humanity. I believe its mostly positive, but I won't pretend that AI doesn't come with its issues. Training models is energy intensive, and the data they are trained on is often scraped without consent. There's the fear of AI displacing many white collar jobs. And the nonzero channce that AI wipes us all out. That is the dark side of AI that we must all reckon with. In this post, I want to keep it light hearted and talk about my experience with LLMs like ChatGPT and Claude, but it's important ackowledge that this technology is weird and has its faults.

## AI as a Hyperfocus Trap

In 2023, I tried out ChatGPT for the first time. I gave it a small task. Make me a bash script to automatically backup some files. I was blown away to see that it not only produced a script that was valid bash, but it also predicted that I would want to keep multiple backups that were timestamped. I began to use AI more after that to automate other small parts of the code I was writing. 

Fast forward to 2026, I barely touch code anymore. That sentence scares me because half of my life has been dedicated to being a programmer. But the truth is, Codex and Claude Code are both faster at writing code and debugging issues than I am on my own. 

But this has led to a new issue I am struggling with. Claude Code and Codex are sold as subscriptions -- You get X tokens to use in a 5 hour period and then X to use every week. Your tokens don't rollover -- So any unused tokens can be thought of as loss. This has led to the term "Claude Maxing" -- Basically trying to use all the tokens you paid for. 

Claude Maxing is not great for the ADHD brain. I'm not saying that getting your money's worth isn't worth it. I'm also not shaming building more with AI. But it can take over your life if you don't find a way to balance it. I have spent many Saturdays glued to my screen. *Just 1 more prompt* I tell myself, *then I'll switch over the laundry*. Then to get the most out of your subscritions, you open up a second session -- the first one was taking too long and you have a new idea. Then a third. Before you know it, you're managing several AI coding sessions at once, directing them like a traffic controller.
